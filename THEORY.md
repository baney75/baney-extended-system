# The theory behind the Baney Extended System

Most filing systems ask what a thing is about. This one asks what a thing asks of you. That single change is the whole system, and everything below explains why it holds.

## 1. Sort by the claim, not the subject

In 1835, at Gilleleje, the young Kierkegaard wrote: "What I really need is to get clear about what I must do, not what I must know." A life's records are not a library. Each one makes a claim on the person who holds it. A bill asks to be paid. A letter from your mother asks to be kept. A chemistry handout asks to be understood. An ad asks for nothing you owe.

So the rooms are verbs:

| Room | The claim it makes | Mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do | Something is asked of you. | `>` |
| Keep | This is part of your story. | `<` |
| Know | This is the world's knowledge, gathered by you. | `=` |
| Let Go | Nothing is owed. | `x` |

A subject can live in every room at once. Chemistry notes are in Know. The lab report you owe is in Do. When it comes back graded, it moves to Keep. The subject did not change; its claim on you did.

## 2. Three powers, one life

Augustine, in *De Trinitate* X.11, finds three powers in the mind: memory, understanding and will. He insists they are "not three lives, but one life." The rooms follow him:

- **Keep** is memory: what happened, to whom, and when.
- **Know** is understanding: what is true regardless of who learned it.
- **Do** is will: what you are turned toward.

Modern memory science draws the same line. Endel Tulving (1972) separated episodic memory, the record of your own life, from semantic memory, knowledge of the world. A voicemail from a friend and a journal article are both "information," but they are different kinds of thing, and a mind that files them together has to sort them again every time it looks.

## 3. Four is the limit

Nelson Cowan (2001) found that attention holds about four chunks at once, not the seven usually quoted. A system you have to think about while using it has already failed. Four rooms fit in the mind with nothing left over, which is why there is no fifth room and no subfolders beyond one level.

## 4. The extended mind must be trustworthy

Andy Clark and David Chalmers (1998) argued that Otto's notebook is part of Otto's mind, because it is always with him, he trusts it, and he uses it without second-guessing. That is the name of this system and its standard. A record store becomes part of you only when it is reliable. So the Baney Extended System uses plain folders, plain file names and plain mail labels that work on every device without an app, and it never deletes anything on its own.

## 5. Backward and forward

Kierkegaard again, in his journals of 1843: "Life must be understood backwards... it must be lived forwards." Keep looks backward. Do looks forward. Know stands outside the timeline. The Inbox is the present moment, the only place a decision is made, and the machine makes most of those decisions so you do not have to.

## 6. The crowd is untruth

In 1847 Kierkegaard wrote that "the crowd is untruth": truth meets a person as a single individual, not as a member of a mass. Most of what fills an inbox is the crowd talking: marketing, notifications, newsletters, sign-in notices. None of it is addressed to you as you. The system's mail rule follows from this. What stays in the inbox is mail from people and mail that asks something of you. Everything the crowd sends is labeled and set aside.

## 7. A time to cast away

Ecclesiastes 3:6: "A time to get, and a time to lose; a time to keep, and a time to cast away." Paul Ricoeur (2000) wrote of "forgetting held in reserve": a forgetting that is not destruction but rest, recoverable if needed. Let Go is that room. Things in it are out of your way but not gone. They are emptied only when you say so, after a season.

## 8. What the system does not claim

Kierkegaard's *Sickness Unto Death* (1849) describes the self as a relation that relates itself to itself and rests in the power that established it. No archive is a self. The Baney Extended System holds your records; it does not hold you. It is a servant: it sorts, you correct, and the correction is always yours.
