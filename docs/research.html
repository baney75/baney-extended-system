Theories of the Mind

Research foundation for the Baney Extended System. About 90 theories in five families, read for one question: what does each one say about how a person should keep the records of a life? Compiled 2026-11-22 by five research passes plus a quote check. Items marked unverified were not confirmed against a primary text.

What the research says

Across Plato, Augustine, Aquinas, Kierkegaard, the Upanishads, Wang Yangming and modern memory science, ten findings keep returning. These are the ground the system stands on.

  1. Knowing exists for doing. Kierkegaard at Gilleleje (1835): "What I really need is to get clear about what I must do, not what I must know." Wang Yangming: someone who knows and does not act does not yet know. Functionalism: a thing is the role it plays. So sort things by what they ask of you, not by what subject they are about.
  2. Your story and the world's knowledge are different kinds of memory. Tulving (1972) split episodic memory (what happened to me, when, with whom) from semantic memory (facts about the world). Conway (2000) ties episodic memory to the self and its goals. So a letter from your mother and a chemistry handout never belong in the same place.
  3. The mind has three powers: memory, understanding and will. Augustine, De Trinitate X.11: "not three lives, but one life." These map onto Keep (memory), Know (understanding), and Do (will, turned toward others and toward work).
  4. Attention holds about four things, one at a time. Cowan (2001): about four chunks. Nyaya: manas attends to one thing at a time. Baddeley: working memory is small. So use four rooms, ask one question, and handle one item at a time.
  5. Memory comes back through cues, mostly when and who. Encoding specificity (Tulving and Thomson 1973); reconstructive memory (Bartlett 1932); persons as relations (Buber 1923; Menkiti 1984); transactive memory (Wegner). So every name carries a date and the people involved.
  6. Open loops pull at you until they have a next step. Zeigarnik (1927, only partly replicated); Masicampo and Baumeister (2011, not independently replicated): a concrete plan quiets an unfinished goal. So anything owed gets a date and a next step, then it stops nagging.
  7. A good tool disappears, and it has to be trustworthy. Heidegger: ready-to-hand tools recede until they break. Clark and Chalmers (1998): Otto's notebook counts as part of his mind because it is reliable, always at hand and trusted. Risko and Gilbert (2016): people offload memory when the store is cheap and dependable. So use plain folders and labels that work on every device without any app.
  8. Forgetting is a gift when it is held in reserve. Ecclesiastes 3:6: "a time to keep, and a time to cast away." Ricoeur (2000): "forgetting held in reserve." Zhuangzi's fasting of the heart-mind; Yoga Sutra 1.2 on stilling the mind's fluctuations. So Let Go is a real room: recoverable for a season, then emptied when you say.
  9. The archive is not the self. The Upanishadic witness that is never itself seen; Avicenna's Floating Man; the Buddhist aggregates; Searle's Chinese Room; Augustine's "I remember that I have remembered"; Kierkegaard's self as a relation that rests in God. So the system stays human first. Machines read a derived index, AI summaries are labeled as reconstructions with links to their sources, and only you assign meaning.
  10. Life is understood backward and lived forward. Kierkegaard (Journals, 1843). Husserl: every moment holds the just-past (retention) and the about-to-come (protention). Kierkegaard's Repetition: recollection repeats backward; true repetition is recollected forward. So Keep looks back, Do looks forward, and the Inbox is the present moment where you decide.

Why this is new: PARA, GTD, Johnny.Decimal and library schemes sort by nouns (projects, areas, subjects). Kierkegaard's charge against Hegel applies to all of them: "a logical system can be given; but a system of existence cannot be given." These rooms are verbs, the stances of a person who has to act. Pascal anticipated the objection: "Let no one say that I have said nothing new; the arrangement of the subject is new."

What does not help

Integrated information theory, panpsychism, Russellian monism and new mysterianism are serious theories of consciousness, but none of them changes how you should file a voicemail. They are recorded below for completeness and left out of the design.
